## Goal
Validate the need for a student-focused insurance aggregator by capturing who we serve, how they shop today, and where the experience breaks down so we can define the right early features.

## Scope
- Document user personas across core segments (international grad, student with dependents, outbound exchange).
- Map the current journey and the ideal aggregator-driven journey.
- Capture problem validation evidence and key pain points.
- Summarize why existing solutions fail and seed the product backlog for next sprint.
- Align on a clear problem statement the team can rally around.

## User personas
### Persona 1: Peenaz - Graduate Student (International, Single)
- Profile: 32-year-old MAM student from India at Yale.
- Goals: Full health coverage in the US with flexibility for a one-year program.
- Frustrations: Yale Health feels expensive for short programs; unaware of reliable alternatives; confused by deductibles/copay/in-network terms.
- Behavior: Spends hours comparing plans online (Reddit, school forums) and defaults to the university plan because of low confidence.

### Persona 2: Li - Graduate Student with Family
- Profile: 27-year-old engineering PhD student from China; spouse and child in the US.
- Goals: Affordable family coverage with strong pediatric and maternity care.
- Frustrations: University family plans are prohibitively expensive; unclear if state or public assistance applies to international students; no simple way to compare dependent coverage.
- Behavior: Calls insurers and university admins directly, receives inconsistent answers, and often overpays.

### Persona 3: Sofia - Exchange Student (US to Europe)
- Profile: 21-year-old undergraduate heading to France for one semester abroad.
- Goals: Short-term, affordable health insurance that satisfies visa requirements.
- Frustrations: Minimal school guidance; unsure about visa/travel coverage requirements; only needs ~5 months, not a full year.
- Behavior: Searches "student insurance France," lands on generic travel sites that ignore student-specific needs.

## User journeys
**Current journey (painful)**
1) Student receives admission and looks for options - overwhelmed by jargon and network/coverage confusion.  
2) Visits university insurance page - typically only one expensive annual option (e.g., Yale Health) with no prorated or modular coverage.  
3) Searches online - information is scattered; comparisons are not student-focused and may exclude pre-existing conditions or student-specific care (sports medicine, mental health).  
4) Gives up and chooses the university plan - overpays and lacks flexibility for dependents or add-ons (dental, pets, etc.).

**Ideal journey (with the aggregator)**
1) Lands on the platform and fills a simple intake form (program duration, marital status, age, health needs, add-ons such as pet/car/dental).  
2) Receives personalized recommendations with cost, duration, benefits, add-ons, transparent pricing, student reviews/ratings, and plan comparison.  
3) Customizes coverage duration (e.g., 8 months) and selects desired add-ons (e.g., eye or dental).  
4) Clicks apply and is redirected to the provider or completes the application within the aggregator.

## Problem validation
- Personal experience: International students (MBA, PhD, exchange) are forced into expensive plans because transparent comparisons are missing.
- Anecdotal insights: At Yale and other universities, peers complain about being "forced" into high-cost plans due to lack of clear options.
- Market research: Colleges require coverage, but most international-friendly plans target citizens or long-term residents; flexible, short-term, modular student plans are scarce.
- Similar platforms: Travel insurance aggregators exist, but none align with academic timelines, prorated terms, or dependent needs.
- Key pain points: No reliable comparison tool; no prorated/short-term coverage; confusion around dependents/assistance/add-ons; student needs (sports medicine, mental health, visa compliance) are often missing.

## Why existing solutions are not enough
- University insurance: costly, inflexible, and tied to the academic year.
- Commercial travel insurance: often fails visa or institutional requirements; lacks academic-year pricing.
- Comparison websites: tuned for general travelers, not students with dependents or specific health needs.
- Manual approach: relying on forums or admin offices is slow, inconsistent, and error-prone.

## Backlog seeds from the research
- Intake flow capturing duration, marital status, age, health needs, and desired add-ons.
- Recommendation and comparison views with transparent pricing, duration, benefits, and student reviews.
- Configurable coverage windows (e.g., 5-12 months) with toggles for add-ons (dental, eye, pet, car).
- Clear handling of dependents: eligibility, pediatric/maternity coverage, and out-of-pocket expectations.
- Guidance on visa/institution compliance and plain-language explanations of deductibles, copays, and networks.

## Sprint success criteria
- Personas, journeys, and validation captured and shared with the team.
- Agreed problem statement: build a flexible, student-first insurance aggregator that supports dependents, prorated terms, and add-ons.
- Backlog candidates identified for sprint 2 planning.
